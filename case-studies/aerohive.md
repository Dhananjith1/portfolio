# AeroHive — Autonomous Drone Fleet for Disaster Relief

<small>Classification: Technical project-management simulation · Planning model: 4 sprints · Planned work: 89 story points</small>

## Overview

AeroHive is a high-level disaster-relief search concept used to practise project planning for a fleet-based technology scenario where normal mobile or internet connectivity may be unavailable or unreliable.

The exercise focuses on project governance, scope, coordination, and risk planning. It does not claim physical drone deployment, operational flight testing, or real disaster-response use.

## Objective

Build a structured, staged delivery plan for a hypothetical five-drone fleet concept, while identifying the dependencies and safety, reliability, communications, and operational risks that would need specialist review before any real-world deployment.

## Project-management work

- Organised the concept into a four-sprint roadmap.
- Modelled 89 planned story points in ClickUp.
- Used Notion to structure high-level project notes, RAID items, and risk ownership.
- Considered a simulation-first approach before any hardware activity; PX4 SITL was referenced as a possible software-in-the-loop environment.
- Identified connectivity, data integrity, coordination, and fail-safe requirements as areas requiring validation by qualified engineering and safety teams.

## Planning artefacts

The simulation covers a high-level roadmap, scope boundaries, RAID/risk considerations, dependency tracking, and staged validation gates. It does not include operational flight procedures.

## Limitations

The five-drone fleet, four-sprint roadmap, and 89 story points are scenario assumptions and planned work. No physical aircraft, field trial, real emergency operation, or collision-rate measurement is claimed. Any real deployment would require qualified engineering, legal, aviation, and emergency-response review.
